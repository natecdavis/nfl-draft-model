Draft Simulator

Replay a team's draft from the picks it held when the draft began: OK or nix each trade it really made on draft night, and choose your own player at every pick you hold. Other teams draft as they really did. Values are the change in expected Super Bowls over five seasons, in % of one title, for that team's situation on draft night.

Loading the boards and the model…

How it works: at each pick, everyone really taken before it is gone (including this team's own earlier picks, even if you passed on them), and anyone you took is gone at your later picks. Each player you take at a position lowers the value of the next one there; after one QB the QB need is filled, and a flagged QB need counts with its measured chance of being real (for a veteran flagged for age or a below-average season: 39% if he is under contract next year, 68% if his deal expires). Candidates are Arif Hasan's consensus board plus drafted players who weren't on it (valued as ranked just past it, #330). Positions are the consensus board's own labels; a label that often changes position in the NFL (a 5-technique, an edge rusher who becomes an off-ball linebacker, a safety who moves to linebacker) is valued as a blend of those positions and shown like "DL/EDGE". Position pricing: the model prices each season at what veterans at that position earn for that role; the other defensible choice prices each position family to match its own later pay, which marks down rotational positions (RB, TE, EDGE, DL) and favors OL. The model's top choice is labeled firm when it is also the top choice under that pricing, and "depends on position pricing" when the position changes (17% of round-1 picks, about 40% later, 2014-2022, mostly EDGE / WR / TE vs OL). Room check (information only; the ranking ignores it): the share of each position's typical snaps (median per team-season, 2014-2025) held by players a rookie is unlikely to displace: the team's good incumbents (top third of DARKO-for-PFF talent at the position after last season; last season's snaps by players still on the team, plus veterans added before the draft), middling incumbents counted at 60% (in past drafts they held rookies back about 60% as much as good ones; weak ones not at all, and pay and draft capital added nothing), plus the expected first-season share of players you take here. Shown for the top choice's position and any position 90% or more held. Releases and retirements without a new contract can't be detected and a few veterans signed after the draft may appear, so treat it as approximate; using it to discount picks didn't improve past drafts (README "Quality-weighted roster need"). Kickers and punters are valued by where they were actually drafted (the board rarely ranks them): the gain of a drafted specialist over an undrafted one, measured in points 2006-2022, and only the first at each position counts; long snappers aren't valued. Draft-night trades: only the trades the team really made can be offered (declined offers aren't public); each comes up before the first pick it involves, with the trade calculator's verdict (the pick on the clock is valued, for the team that gets it, as the player really taken there: his board rank and position were known when the deal was made) and the market price, and one you can no longer make after an earlier choice is skipped. If you nix a trade, the partner's slot is filled by the rule at the top: by default, whoever was really picked there (the board is then exactly what it really was at every later pick); or, as the other end of the range, the partner makes the choices it really made with our picks, and the player really taken at that slot goes back on the board. "Delivered" columns count each player's first four seasons; drafts since 2023 count the seasons played so far (through 2025). Undrafted players count from the 2016 class.
